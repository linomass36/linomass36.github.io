How it is put together

A place can hold several panels

Tabs across the top of a place move between its panels. Some places keep all their panels in one file; others keep them as separate pages and show the same tab bar on each. You cannot tell from using it, which is the point — the difference exists because merging some of them would have risked breaking them, not because it means anything to you.

Red means owed

Arterial red means something wants you today, and it means nothing else here. Links are the blue-grey. The previous version used one colour for both, which is why a board of thirteen tiles read as an emergency in which nothing could be prioritised.

Names, two ways

The pages have written names — the Standing, the Examiner, Recalibrate. The drawer has a Plain names switch that swaps them for plain ones and keeps the written name underneath, so you can learn one from the other. It syncs between your devices.

Nothing was deleted

Pages that were folded into a place still exist at their old address and send you to the right panel, so an old bookmark or a link in a journal entry still works. The v1 planning documents are kept and labelled in the Archive.

If something goes wrong

Back it up, and know what a backup holds

Settings → Backup writes every store in this browser to one file, and reads one back. It reads what is actually there rather than a list someone has to remember to update.

Three things are deliberately left out: sync bookkeeping, the device-only vault key, and your calendar token. A downloaded file is less private than the cloud, not more, and a credential does not belong in one. Sign in to the calendar again after a restore.

Restoring is two steps. The file is described first — how many stores, from when, how many would be overwritten and which — and only then are the buttons offered. Merge adds only what is missing; Replace overwrites. Neither deletes a store the file does not mention.
