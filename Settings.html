Fair question, and the last version had no answer — it only ever showed the effect on today. Here is the same fortnight run twice, on the one number that makes the case by itself: a 250-day streak with 250 cards behind it.

The mechanism, in three moves

01
You name what is blocked Not a mood — a constraint, at a strength, ticked activity by activity. A lower back that rules out lifting and a long sit is a different fact from one that rules out standing up, and the picker asks which. It has no opinion about recall either way.
02

03

Three strengths, because most days are not the worst day

Everything modelled on this page is the strongest of the three. It is the right setting for a fortnight in a sling and the wrong one for a sore shoulder, and having only it is what made the mechanism something you reached for twice a year.

NOTED
Just keep note Nothing is held and nothing is lowered. The board asks for everything and every number reads as it would on any other day. It is on the record so that a thin week, read back in a month, has its reason attached to it.
SLOWER
Take it slower The whole board stays on screen, in full, with the real numbers — nothing hidden and nothing folded. Only the size of a finished day moves: the small version of each affected system clears it. Not a floor day, and not modelled below.
STOPS ME
Debilitating The version the fortnight below runs. Systems held, nothing accruing, one ask for the day — and the numbers still reachable in one tap, because being held is a fact about what is owed rather than a reason to be shown nothing.

Before any plan: what does the day actually cost?

Two versions of this page got this wrong in opposite directions. The first offered a clearing plan without checking whether the day was payable. The second checked, but counted in cards — and cards are not comparable to anything. Minutes are. A card is only heavy or light once you know what it costs, and the moment the load is priced in minutes the argument usually settles itself.

The same fortnight, three ways

First, a thing the model says that you should hear: your back does not hold Anki. A lumbar injury rules out lifting, running and a long sit — it has no opinion whatsoever about recall, so the scoping leaves the queue owed. That is the model being right rather than lenient, and it is the whole reason scoping exists. Column B below therefore assumes a condition that does cover recall — Flat, the one for a five-day rut. Declaring the injury alone would hold the grind board and change nothing here.

Which points at something bigger: the 250-card backlog is not a conditions problem at all. Conditions stop it compounding while you are down. They do not fix what built it. That is the section below the table, and it is the one that actually matters.

The part that is doing the damage

The streak counts 250 days and says nothing about what happened on any of them. That is not a neutral omission. The cheapest way to keep the number alive is to skim 25 cards, and skimming defers the rest — so the streak is what is producing the backlog. The instrument is eating the thing it was built to protect, and it will keep doing that no matter how the fortnight above goes.

The repair is small: the hub records what kind of day each one was, and shows the two numbers together — never the streak on its own. A day can be a clear, a skim, or a held day. All three keep the streak. Only one of them is a lie, and once it is labelled it stops being an invisible one.

What to do with the 250

On holding and Anki, honestly: the hub cannot reach into your collection, and cards do not evaporate because a page went quiet. Holding means one concrete action — postpone the due queue by the length of the hold — which the hub tells you to take and records. Postponing costs a little retention on those cards. Skimming 25 a day costs retention and grows the debt, which is the trade you are currently making by default.

When it lifts

The condition carries a review date — seven days for an injury — so it cannot quietly become permanent. On the day it expires the hub asks one question: still true? Yes extends it and says so on the record. No starts the ramp, which goes up one rung at a time and only when the previous rung was met. The full board comes back when you have had an ordinary day, and what counts as ordinary is the hub's arithmetic, not your mood on the morning.
