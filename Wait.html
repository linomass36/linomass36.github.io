Two rules that are not about her

Do not soothe the wait by texting the one you let go

That door was closed on purpose and closed kindly. Reopening it because this one is quiet is the hedge coming back under a different name — and it would be a worse version of it, because now she knows what the first message was worth.

If she writes late, do not answer on impulse

A reply after silence is not yet an answer. The thing to find out is whether she wants to have the conversation, which is a separate question from whether she typed something back — and it is not one a fast reply is capable of asking.

Why this is written down

The question that started it was whether lining up someone new while reaching back to someone old was a hedge. It was — not because a date existed, but because the date was being kept warm as the thing to land on if the first ask failed. Two doors held open is one person deciding and two people waiting.

So one door was closed, plainly and without making her carry the reason. What is left is the other one, and the only work left in it is not sending things. That is all this page does: it counts, it says what the hour permits, and it refuses to invent a fifth message.

A no arrives by silence too. There is no text that converts silence into a cleaner answer; every candidate for that text is another ask wearing a coat.
